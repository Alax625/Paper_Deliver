PACT: A Pragmatic Prompting Framework for Expressive and Voice-Preserving Speech-to-Speech Translation

作者：Yuchen Song, Satoshi Nakamura

语音翻译评分 19speechvoicespeech translation查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

PACT提出目标空间语用提示框架，将源语音的语用线索映射到目标语言表达空间并校准，以连续提示驱动冻结的语音生成器，实现表达和声音保持的语音到语音翻译。

解决了什么问题

现有端到端S2ST、语音MLLM和级联系统难以在目标语言中保留情感、语调、节奏、停顿和语用态度，只能保留内容或音色。

核心方法

PACT提取源语用线索，投影到目标语言表达空间，校准到可实现的目标子空间，并转换为连续提示用于冻结的语音生成器。

主要创新点

提出目标空间语用提示框架，将源语用线索映射到目标语言表达空间并校准；使用连续提示驱动冻结语音生成器，避免微调。

实验结论

在双向西班牙语-英语TOPI评估中，PACT在内容保真度、声音保持和表达保持指标上取得最佳总体性能。

可能局限

摘要未提及具体局限。

适合谁读

语音翻译、语音合成、跨语言表达迁移研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要提及现有端到端S2ST、语音MLLM和级联系统，但未列出具体文献。

对研究的启发

将源语用线索投影到目标空间并校准到可实现子空间的做法，可启发其他跨语言副语言特征迁移任务。

是否值得精读

针对语用保持难点且取得最佳性能，值得深入阅读。

Fish Audio Drama 3 Technical Report

作者：Dawei Han, Shidong Li, Shijia Liao, Jiahua Liu, Songting Liu, Qingzheng Wang, Yuxuan Wang, Xin Chen, Yifan Cheng, Tianyu Li, Gangin Park, Xuehao Yang, Chang Zeng, Haoyang Zhang, Ruoyi Zhang, Xiangyu Zhang

其他评分 16speechaudiovoiceacoustic查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

Fish Audio Drama 3是一个开源语音生成模型，基于35B MoE骨干，支持多参考声音克隆、自然语言指导、多轮修订、场景配音、填充编辑和流式合成。

解决了什么问题

需要一种能根据文本生成有指导表演、支持多任务且高效推理的语音生成模型。

核心方法

采用35B参数MoE骨干（每token激活3B），混合线性注意力，新颖的码本多token预测音频解码器；训练使用三阶段预训练、指令微调和大批量强化学习（仅用人类偏好模型作为奖励）。

主要创新点

在单一模型中集成多轮修订、场景配音、填充编辑和流式文本输入；新码本多token预测解码器；混合线性注意力；仅用人类偏好模型作为奖励的大批量强化学习。

实验结论

在H200上单流解码速度为271帧/秒，对比Fish Audio S2的177帧/秒；摘要未提供其他实验评估。

可能局限

摘要未提及具体局限。

适合谁读

语音生成、文本到语音、音频合成研究者。

和已有工作的关系

对研究的启发

DualAffect: Dual-Path Paralinguistic Integration for Generative Audiovisual Emotion Understanding

作者：Yibo Wang, Jisheng Dang, Bimei Wang, Junhong Liu, Jiongpei Hou, Jingwen Zhao, Wenjun Ma, Hong Peng, Bin Hu, Qi Tian, Tat-Seng Chua

其他评分 15audioacousticspeaker查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

DualAffect提出双路径架构，通过融合路径（视觉条件声学特征）和直接路径（时序组织声学token）整合副语言信息到共享语言解码器，提升生成式视听情感理解。

解决了什么问题

连接副语言线索与生成答案具有挑战性，尤其当声音表达与视觉外观不一致时。

核心方法

双路径架构：融合路径通过视觉查询关联声音与视觉内容，直接路径将有效声学序列压缩为有序token；两条路径复用相同声学编码，共享语言解码器；混合任务适应支持情感识别和通用视听问答。

主要创新点

双路径设计让解码器同时获取联合视听上下文和按自身时间线组织的声学证据；混合任务适应；通过路径干预分析验证直接声学证据的作用。

实验结论

在CA-MER上平均指标67.89，EmotionTalk测试集准确率62.88%，Daily-Omni准确率49.87%；零化直接token导致EmotionTalk验证准确率降9.75个百分点，CA-MER平均指标降2.66点；最大CA-MER子集效应出现在总体情感与音频一致但与视觉不一致时。

可能局限

摘要未提及具体局限。

适合谁读

多模态情感计算、视听理解、生成模型研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要未提及具体相关系统，仅指出连接副语言线索与生成答案的挑战。

对研究的启发

声学表示组织作为设计轴，双路径融合与直接路径的结合思路。

是否值得精读

有定量结果和消融分析，代码开源，值得深入阅读。

Known-Voice Cloning and the Limits of Personality Transfer for Character Design

作者：Johanna Magdalena Kuch, Maud van Lier, Daksitha Senel Withanage Don, Patrick Gebhard, Tanja Schneeberger, Elisabeth André

语音合成评分 14voicespeaker查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

通过两项研究检验已知声音克隆是否将人格印象转移到虚拟角色，发现人格转移证据有限，效果选择性且未跨特质或情境泛化。

解决了什么问题

声音克隆可能将熟悉声音的人格印象转移到虚拟角色，引发操纵担忧；但缺乏直接测试人格转移是否系统性发生。

核心方法

两项研究：研究1在大学环境使用讲师克隆声音；研究2在线使用社交媒体创作者声音；基于身份激活、特质转移和人物重建概念分析。

主要创新点

直接测试已知声音克隆的人格转移，而非仅研究熟悉度或个性化；结合定量与定性分析。

实验结论

两项研究中人格转移证据有限，效果选择性，未跨特质或研究情境泛化；在线研究中声音身份线索激活期望但未重建整体人物印象；定性显示判断受感知人工性和声形匹配影响。

可能局限

摘要指出证据有限和效果不泛化，但未明确其他局限。

适合谁读

声音克隆、人机交互、虚拟角色设计、心理学研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要提及先前工作主要考察熟悉度和个性化效果，而非直接测试人格转移。

对研究的启发

提示声音克隆的人格转移并非必然，需要考虑人工性和声形匹配等因素。

是否值得精读

对声音克隆的社会影响和虚拟角色设计有实证启示，值得深入阅读。

Self-Supervised Source-Filter Model of Speech Production

作者：Nani Meka, Raghavan Ramesh, Sri Rama Murty Kodukula

自监督学习评分 12speechself-supervised查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

SoFilNet是一个自监督可微分源-滤波器网络，直接从原始波形估计语音产生参数（源和滤波器），仅通过重构训练，无需标注。

解决了什么问题

深度学习语音模型很少表示源-滤波器产生过程，依赖无物理对应的潜在特征。

核心方法

估计源参数（F0、浊音、五个频带的非周期性）和滤波器参数（有序共振峰极点、附加极点、反谐振零点）；仅训练重构输入语音；使用自相关伪标签（用模型自身滤波器细化）、周期分数和谱对比度训练F0、浊音、非周期性和共振峰锐度。

主要创新点

自监督估计语音产生参数，无需标注；使用自相关伪标签和频谱对比度损失。

实验结论

在VTR-FORMANTS上估计共振峰达到报告方法中最低平均误差（包括监督跟踪器）；在未见PTDB-TUG语料库上F0帧误差与最佳经典跟踪器匹配，低于所有学习跟踪器；参数符合语音产生理论（非周期性随频率上升，零点在鼻辅音加深等）。

可能局限

摘要未提及具体局限。

适合谁读

语音产生建模、自监督学习、语音分析研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要提及现有深度学习模型依赖潜在特征，与监督跟踪器和学习跟踪器比较，但未列出具体文献。

对研究的启发

可微分源-滤波器建模思路，自监督训练伪标签设计。

是否值得精读

结果优秀且参数符合物理理论，但相关度较低，可选择性阅读。

PACT: A Pragmatic Prompting Framework for Expressive and Voice-Preserving Speech-to-Speech Translation2610.18247v1语音翻译
展开摘要

Speech-to-speech translation should preserve not only what is said, but also who says it and how it is said. Existing end-to-end S2ST, speech MLLM, and cascade systems can preserve content or timbre, but often struggle to realize emotion, tone, rhythm, pauses, and pragmatic attitude in the target language. We propose PACT, a target-space pragmatic prompting framework for expressive and voice-preserving S2ST. PACT extracts source pragmatic cues, projects them into the target-language expression space, calibrates the projected representation to a realizable target subspace, and converts it into continuous prompts for a frozen speech generator. On bidirectional Spanish-English TOPI evaluation, PACT achieves the best overall performance among evaluated systems across content fidelity, voice preservation, and expression preservation metrics.

Fish Audio Drama 3 Technical Report2610.18010v1其他
展开摘要

We introduce Fish Audio Drama 3, an open-source speech generation model that turns text into directed performances, built on a 35B-parameter mixture-of-experts backbone that activates 3B parameters per token. It clones voices from multiple references, follows natural-language direction, revises a take when the next turn gives a note, voices multi-character scenes, edits existing speech by infilling, and synthesizes long-form and streaming audio. Multi-turn revision, scene dubbing, editing by infilling, and streaming text input are new in Drama 3, all in one model. Drama 3 pairs the hybrid linear-attention backbone with a new codebook multi-token-prediction audio decoder that predicts the acoustic codebooks of each frame in dedicated layers that attend over the entire sequence. The model is trained with a three-stage pre-training recipe, instruction fine-tuning, and large-batch reinforcement learning whose only reward is a single learned model of human preference. Its inference engine decodes a single stream at 271 frames per second on one H200, against 177 for Fish Audio S2, although Drama 3 has more than seven times as many parameters. The model weights are available on GitHub (https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech) and Hugging Face (https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/drama-3), and inference is available through SGLang-Omni (https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-omni). We highly encourage readers to visit https://fish.audio to direct their own performances.

DualAffect: Dual-Path Paralinguistic Integration for Generative Audiovisual Emotion Understanding2610.18073v1其他
展开摘要

Understanding human emotion is essential for multimodal systems that interpret both the content of an interaction and the speaker's expressive state. However, connecting paralinguistic cues to generated answers remains challenging, particularly when vocal expression diverges from visual appearance. We propose DualAffect, a dual-path architecture that integrates visually conditioned acoustic features and temporally organized acoustic tokens into a shared language decoder. The two pathways reuse the same acoustic encoding. The fusion pathway associates sound with visual content through visual queries, while the direct pathway compresses the valid acoustic sequence into ordered tokens through an independent projector. This design gives the decoder access to both joint audiovisual context and acoustic evidence organized along its own timeline. Mixed-task adaptation with task-dependent output preservation supports emotion recognition and general audiovisual question answering within the same model. DualAffect achieves an average metric of 67.89 on CA-MER and accuracies of 62.88% on the EmotionTalk test set and 49.87% on Daily-Omni. Pathway interventions show that the trained decoder uses direct acoustic evidence alongside existing fusion. With model parameters and fusion inputs fixed, zeroing the direct tokens reduces EmotionTalk validation accuracy by 9.75 percentage points and the CA-MER average metric by 2.66 points. The largest CA-MER subset effect occurs when the overall emotion agrees with audio but differs from vision. These findings identify acoustic representation organization as a practical design axis for connecting paralinguistic perception to unified audiovisual generation. Code is available at https://github.com/YiboWang2002/DualAffect.

Known-Voice Cloning and the Limits of Personality Transfer for Character Design2610.18669v1语音合成
展开摘要

Recent advances in zero-shot voice cloning systems make it possible to reproduce a recognizable voice from a short recording and attach it to arbitrary virtual characters. Prior work suggests that familiar vocal identity can carry over associated personality impressions to new targets such as robots or virtual characters. However, these works have mainly examined familiarity and personalization effects rather than directly testing whether a cloned familiar voice systematically transfers personality impressions to an embodied virtual character. Such transference raises the concern that cloned voices could be used to manipulate people into engaging with virtual characters as capable social actors. Across two studies, we investigated whether personality impressions transfer when a virtual character speaks with the cloned voice of a familiar person. Study 1 examined this question in a real-world university setting using the cloned voice of a lecturer familiar to the participating students. Study 2 examined the same question online using voices of social media content creators and members of their communities. Drawing on the concepts of identity activation, trait transfer, and person reconstruction, we show that evidence of personality transfer was limited in both studies. Observed effects were selective and did not generalize across traits or study contexts. In the online study in particular, recognizable voice identity cues activated expectations about the speaker but did not reconstruct the broader person impression. Qualitative responses further indicated that judgments were frequently shaped by perceived artificiality and by whether voice and appearance fit together. Our findings moderate common concerns that cloned voices straightforwardly manipulate people into perceiving artificial characters as capable social actors.

Self-Supervised Source-Filter Model of Speech Production2610.18120v1自监督学习
展开摘要

Deep learning models of speech seldom represent the source-filter process by which speech is produced, relying instead on latent features with no physical correspondence to articulation or phonation. We propose SoFilNet, a self-supervised, differentiable Source-Filter Network that estimates the parameters of speech production directly from the raw waveform: a source, specified by fundamental frequency (F0), voicing and the aperiodicity of five frequency bands, and a filter, specified by ordered formant poles, additional poles that shape the rest of the spectral envelope, and antiresonance zeros. The model is trained only to reconstruct the input speech; no formant or pitch annotation is used. Since per-bin spectral losses favor blurred spectra wherever the model is uncertain, we train F0 and voicing against autocorrelation pseudo-labels refined with the model's own filter, aperiodicity against the periodic fraction of each band, and formant sharpness against spectral contrast. On VTR-FORMANTS, the estimated formants attain the lowest mean error among the reported methods, including supervised trackers, and on the unseen PTDB-TUG corpus the F0 frame error matches the best classical tracker and is lower than that of all learned trackers. The parameters are also consistent with speech production theory: aperiodicity rises with frequency and orders phone classes by the turbulence of their excitation, and the zeros deepen on nasal consonants, the lower one rising with place of articulation.

PhonoVoxtLM: Exploring Single-Codebook Phonological Tokens for Speech Understanding and Generation2610.18469v1语音识别
展开摘要

We investigate how speech token representations can support both understanding and generation within a unified speech language model. We present PhonoVoxtLM, built on the existing VoxtLM framework using phonological tokens designed to retain linguistic content and prosodic information. The existing tokenizer is jointly trained for speech recognition and resynthesis. Its single-codebook units serve as both speech inputs and generation targets, without additional speech-token prediction modules. Experiments show improvements over HuBERT-based baselines across understanding and generation under different training-data scales. Further comparisons with WavLM-based tokens reveal trade-offs between recognition and synthesis, highlighting the importance of evaluating shared representations across both capabilities. We further extend the framework to spoken question answering through fine-tuning with replay. The adapted model improves answering performance over similarly adapted HuBERT baselines while retaining advantages on the original speech tasks. Our findings support single-codebook representations designed for both content and prosody as a practical choice for unified multitask speech modeling.

Estimating Surprisal from the Speech Signal2610.18439v1语音合成
展开摘要

Although language is predominantly spoken, empirical tests of surprisal theory rely almost entirely on written text. In practice, surprisal is estimated using language models (LMs) trained on text and applied to transcriptions, creating a text bottleneck that limits evaluation of the theory in the spoken modality. We remove this bottleneck by estimating surprisal directly from the speech signal using a speech LM. This raises a key question: should surprisal be defined over realization-specific acoustic inputs or over abstract linguistic units? Since a single unit can have many distinct acoustic realizations, each assigned a different probability by the model, defining surprisal over abstract units is difficult. To address this, we introduce an importance sampling estimator that approximates the probability of an abstract unit by marginalizing over realizations generated by a text-to-speech model. Using pupillometry data from a listening study, we evaluate surprisal theory in this text-free setting at multiple levels of granularity. Obtaining surprisal from several speech LMs, we consistently find that surprisal estimated via importance sampling better predicts pupil dilation than realization-specific surprisal for words. We also find that surprisal values for sub-lexical units are predictive of pupil dilation. While we find that surprisal can be estimated without recourse to text, and that marginalizing over distinct realizations of words is more predictive of lexical processing effort, surprisal as a whole is not a strong predictor of change in pupil size, regardless of its source.

Audio memory must recognise recurring sources across acoustic changes while keeping their attributes up to date. Overall accuracy does not reveal how these demands interact. Supervised fine tuning (SFT) is commonly monitored through training and evaluation loss, but loss does not fully characterize how model functionality evolves along the same trajectory. We study this gap through dialect adaptation of Qwen3-ASR, MiMo-Audio, and FireRed-LLM across learning rates from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-3}$. With the rapid development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), evaluating their video understanding capabilities has become increasingly important. We introduce OmniCompass, a holistic benchmark for omni-modal video grounding and understanding. OmniCompass defines two evaluation tracks: OmniCompass-VL assesses vision-language models on tasks requiring visual evidence, while OmniCompass-AV assesses omni-modal models on tasks requiring joint audio-visual evidence. Each track includes three complementary tasks: temporal grounding (TG), spatial grounding (SG), and question answering (QA), assessing when a referenced target appears, where it is, and what happens. Multimodal Emotion Recognition (MER) aims to understand human emotions by integrating information from multiple modalities such as speech and text. Psychological studies suggest that emotions exhibit latent hierarchical structured relationships in their semantic representations. However, most MER approaches rely on Euclidean representations, which capture fine-grained cross-modal interactions but do not explicitly provide a geometric prior for organizing hierarchical emotional structures. To mitigate this gap, we propose GeoMER, a joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic modeling framework that introduces hyperbolic emotion-core routing to capture higher-level, abstract emotional representations for MER. The proposed framework jointly models token-level cross-modal semantic interactions and latent emotion representations in two complementary geometric spaces. Specifically, GeoMER consists of a Euclidean Multimodal Fusion Module (EuclFusion) for multimodal representation fusion and a Hyperbolic Geometric Core Routing (HypGeoRoute) module that performs geometry-aware core assignment and aggregation in a hyperbolic manifold to capture more abstract feature representations. The HypGeoRoute module complements the Euclidean token-level modeling.
