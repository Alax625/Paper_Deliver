A richness-sensitive Acoustic Biodiversity Index from species-level detections: classifier benchmarking, false-positive control and validation against standard soundscape indices in an African savanna and an urban zoo

作者：Muhammad Saeed, Dmitry Mikhaylov, Mohamed H. Alhosani, Yasser F. Al Wahedi, Rory Pilossof, Ahmed Arabi, Walid Shaaba

其他评分 6acoustic查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

本文提出并验证了一种基于物种级别检测的声学生物多样性指数（ABI），通过分类器基准测试、误报控制和与标准声学指数的比较，显示其与物种丰富度高度相关。

解决了什么问题

传统声学指数总结声能分布而非物种，通常不能良好跟踪物种丰富度，需要更可解释的生物多样性指标。

核心方法

修订并测试基于物种级别检测的声学生物多样性指数ABI；使用BirdNET embeddings + logistic regression分类器进行物种检测；应用位置和季节过滤器以及事后位置过滤移除不可能物种；通过昼夜匹配稀疏化标准化努力；计算ABI_E（归一化Shannon多样性 H / ln S_ref）和稳定性项T；与六个标准声学指数比较。

主要创新点

从物种级别检测构建指数；显式误报控制；声明区域参考库；努力标准化；展示与丰富度强相关性。

实验结论

在分类器基准测试中，BirdNET embeddings + logistic regression表现最佳（macro-F1 0.750±0.029），随机帧分割虚增准确率从0.41到0.70；在实际环境中，稀树草原站点有21物种（ABI_E=0.38），动物园站点1.3-13物种（ABI_E=0.03-0.21）；ABI_E与丰富度Spearman rho=0.93，高于标准指数（最佳ACI rho=0.48）；稳定性项在稀树草原较低（0.39）而动物园较高（0.65-0.67），显示其惩罚自然昼夜活动周期。

TurnTurk-GPT: Text-Based Turn-Taking Prediction for Turkish

作者：Ahmet Tuğrul Bayrak, Fatma Nur Korkmaz, Nur Yağmur Tuncer, Mustafa Sertaç Türkel

其他评分 6speechspeaker查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

本文提出并评估了基于土耳其语GPT-2微调的文本轮次预测模型TurnTurk-GPT，显示其优于现有多语言检测器，并揭示了动词形态在轮次结束预测中的作用。

解决了什么问题

针对土耳其语对话的轮次预测任务，在流式无标点、小写文本上评估不同方法的性能。

核心方法

对土耳其语GPT-2模型使用TurnGPT目标在合成和真实对话上微调；在词级决策点上评估，使用记录分离和说话人分离交叉验证、嵌套阈值选择和配对聚类自助法测试；与规则基线、零样本语言模型和多语言轮次结束检测器比较；通过移除后缀分析形态线索。

主要创新点

提出针对土耳其语的文本轮次预测模型；结合合成与真实对话微调；发现动词结尾形态是关键线索；公开发布模型和评估协议。

实验结论

TurnTurk-GPT在记录分离和说话人分离评估下均优于多语言检测器；仅在测试说话人出现在训练中时优于零样本语言模型，未见说话人上两者无显著差异；移除轮次最后单词后缀使AP降低最多44%，而句子内部单词仅2%；仅合成对话训练的模型最弱；当说话人停顿时，零样本和多语言基线接近随机水平。

A richness-sensitive Acoustic Biodiversity Index from species-level detections: classifier benchmarking, false-positive control and validation against standard soundscape indices in an African savanna and an urban zoo2610.42042v1其他
展开摘要

Acoustic indices are widely used as rapid proxies for biodiversity, but they summarise the distribution of sound energy rather than the species producing it and often track richness poorly. We revise and test an Acoustic Biodiversity Index (ABI) built from species-level detections. Its primary form, ABI_E, is Shannon diversity of detections normalised by the regional species pool expected at the site and season (H / ln S_ref); temporal stability T is reported as a separate component. We first benchmarked four classifiers on a strongly labelled tropical dataset (1,216 calls, 24 classes) with recording-grouped cross-validation. BirdNET embeddings + logistic regression performed best (macro-F1 0.750 +/- 0.029), and random frame-level splits inflated accuracy from 0.41 to 0.70. We then applied BirdNET v2.4 with a location and season filter to recordings from a riverine savanna in the Zambezi Valley, Zimbabwe, and from three habitats at Emirates Park Zoo and Resort, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. A stricter post-hoc location filter removed 517 high-confidence detections of 9 implausible species, all at the zoo. After diel-matched rarefaction to equal effort (6 h), the savanna site held 21 species (Hill number 1D = 9.83; ABI_E = 0.38) against 1.3-13 species (ABI_E = 0.03-0.21) at the zoo sites. Across 149 recorded hours, ABI_E correlated strongly with richness (Spearman rho = 0.93), whereas six standard indices did not (best: ACI, rho = 0.48). The stability term was lower at the savanna site (0.39) than at the zoo (0.65-0.67), showing that it penalises the natural diel cycle of bird activity. Species-level indices with explicit false-positive control, a declared regional reference pool and effort standardisation provide a more interpretable biodiversity indicator than energy-based acoustic indices.

TurnTurk-GPT: Text-Based Turn-Taking Prediction for Turkish2610.42010v1其他
展开摘要

This paper presents TurnTurk-GPT, a text-based turn-taking predictor for Turkish obtained by fine-tuning a Turkish GPT-2 model with the TurnGPT objective on synthetic and real dialogue, and evaluates it on real Turkish conversation against rule baselines, a zero-shot Turkish language model and a widely used multilingual end-of-turn detector. Evaluation uses word-level decision points on unpunctuated, lower-cased text approximating the output of streaming speech recognisers, recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint folds, nested threshold selection and paired cluster bootstrap tests. TurnTurk-GPT outperforms the multilingual detector under both recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint evaluation. It outperforms the zero-shot language model only when the test speakers occur in training; on unseen speakers the two are statistically indistinguishable. Removing the suffixes of the turn-final word reduces AP by up to 44%, against 2% for a sentence-internal word, locating the Turkish end-of-turn cue in verb-final morphology. Synthetic dialogue alone yields the weakest trained model, and when a speaker pauses the zero-shot and multilingual baselines remain close to chance. The model and the evaluation protocol are publicly released.

A General Theoretical Framework for Transformed Geometric Pattern Matching in Music2610.42095v1其他
展开摘要

In this paper, we present a framework of concepts, terminology and notation for geometric pattern matching, centred around the idea of a {\em transformation class}. After formally defining the concept of a transformation class, we provide detailed definitions of a number of specific transformation classes that are particularly relevant to pattern matching and pattern discovery in music, which is the primary application domain within which the framework has been developed. We then provide formal definitions of the notions of {\em match}, {\em occurrence}, {\em maximal match\/} and {\em maximal occurrence\/} and prove some results relating to these concepts. We then define and explore in detail the idea of an {\em occurrence set\/} and the more general notion of a {\em heterogeneous occurrence set}. Finally, we explore in detail the concept of {\em transformational equivalence}, including a consideration of {\em transformation groups}, along with the group-theoretic and transformational equivalence properties of selected transformation classes.
