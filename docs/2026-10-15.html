Soloist: A Generative Model for Blind Voice Separation in the Wild

作者：Chen Yang, ChuFan Yu, Botian Jiang, Jingqi Chen, Wenxuan Wang, Yitian Gong, Zhaoye Fei, Xipeng Qiu

其他评分 22speechaudiomusicvoiceacousticsinging查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

提出Soloist，一个以混合音频为条件自回归生成个体语音流的盲语音分离生成模型，无需目标说话人查询或预定义语音数量，并通过两阶段训练适应真实录音。

解决了什么问题

真实世界语音分离需从重叠语音、噪声和音乐中恢复个体声音，但孤立源录音稀缺，且语音数量未知。

核心方法

自回归生成音频编解码序列作为个体语音流；两阶段训练：第一阶段合成混合监督分离与真实录音说话人时间线预测辅助任务，第二阶段基于模型评判器的强化学习。

主要创新点

无需目标说话人查询或预定义语音数量；利用真实录音的辅助任务和强化学习减少对孤立源的依赖；引入模型评判器提供奖励。

实验结论

摘要报告Soloist在多种场景下恢复干净语音、从音乐中提取人声并保持相对较高的音高准确度和可懂度，在未知语音数量真实录音上分离良好，并集成到全双工对话流水线中展示实用性。

摘要未明确说明局限性。

适合谁读

从事盲语音分离、生成式语音处理、对话系统研究的人员。

和已有工作的关系

对研究的启发

可关注利用真实录音进行弱监督或强化学习以减少对孤立源依赖的范式，以及生成式模型在语音分离中的应用。

是否值得精读

由于提出无查询盲分离和两阶段训练策略，并展示在真实对话流水线中的集成，值得深入阅读。

Soloist: A Generative Model for Blind Voice Separation in the Wild2610.19716v1其他
展开摘要

Real-world voice separation must recover individual voices from overlapping speech, environmental noise, and music, yet isolated source recordings are rarely available for supervision. We introduce Soloist, a generative model for blind voice separation that autoregressively generates individual voice streams as audio-codec sequences conditioned on the mixture, without requiring a target-speaker query or a predefined voice count. We propose a two-stage training paradigm to learn from synthetic mixtures and adapt to real recordings. The first stage combines supervised separation on synthetic mixtures with isolated source targets and an auxiliary task that predicts speaker timelines from real recordings, learning who speaks when from natural acoustics. The second stage applies reinforcement learning to real recordings, with rewards supplied by a model judge that compares candidate separations based on voice purity and content preservation without isolated source targets. Across diverse scenarios, Soloist recovers clean speech from multi-speaker overlap and noisy recordings, and extracts singing vocals from music accompaniment while maintaining relatively high pitch accuracy and intelligibility. On real-world recordings with unknown voice counts, Soloist also separates voices well. Finally, we integrate Soloist into a full-duplex conversation processing pipeline that assembles separated outputs into continuous speaker tracks, demonstrating its practical utility in real conversations.

SEA-HELM Audio: Evaluating Multilingual ASR-LLM Pipeline Performance Across SEA-HELM Subsets2610.19869v1语音识别
展开摘要

We introduce SEA-HELM Audio, a Southeast Asian (SEA) multilingual evaluation framework that extends the text-based SEA-HELM benchmark to assess performance degradation in cascaded automatic speech recognition and large language model (ASR-LLM) pipelines. Using synthesized speech, we compare speech-input and text-input performance across five languages (Chinese, English, Malay, Indonesian, and Tamil) on multiple-choice reasoning and instruction-following tasks. We evaluate open-weight and commercial ASR-LLM systems using identical verifiers and quantify the speech gap as the difference between text and speech accuracy. Results show substantial degradation, with accuracy drops of 9.00 to 18.50 percentage points for multiple-choice reasoning and 20.00 to 42.86 points for instruction following. Tamil exhibits the largest degradation, exceeding 40 percentage points. Our findings highlight the limitations of text-only benchmarks in predicting voice-agent reliability and demonstrate the value of SEA-HELM Audio for evaluating multilingual voice-agent systems.

Long-Prompt Zero-Shot TTS: Benchmarking Prosody Reproduction Beyond Voice Cloning2610.19774v1语音合成
展开摘要

Zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) typically conditions on a short speech prompt, which conveys timbre but provides only limited evidence of a speaker's prosodic patterns. We formulate Long-Prompt Zero-Shot TTS, a task that uses long speech prompts at inference time to reproduce speaker-specific prosody without target-speaker fine-tuning. We introduce LP-TTS-Bench, a bilingual benchmark of 37 speakers with 63.95 prompt hours and 2,728 held-out test utterances. Beyond intelligibility and timbre similarity, LP-TTS-Bench evaluates timing, prominence, intonation, and model-based prosody metrics, enabling multidimensional evaluation of prosody reproduction. As a reference system, we develop LPTTS, whose hierarchical Long-Prompt Encoder compresses long speech prompts into a fixed number of continuous prosody vectors. Experiments show improvements in several prosodic dimensions over a short-prompt baseline, with overall gains as prompt duration increases up to approximately 10 minutes and saturation at longer durations.

Repairing Speech Access After Text Updates with Frozen Affine Fields2610.20253v1其他
展开摘要

Speech-capable language models inherit most of their knowledge from text-rich language backbones, and continued model maintenance is likewise dominated by text updates. Knowledge acquired through a text update can nevertheless remain difficult to access when the same query is spoken. We formulate this mismatch as post-update speech access repair, where the knowledge-bearing checkpoint stays frozen and a detachable operator repairs speech-only access in one model pass. We introduce Affine Fields for Interface Repair across Modalities (AFFIRM), which first learns and freezes a base correction for speech hidden states, then adds query-specific corrections with zero mean over the operator-training queries. Paired speech and text states provide the coordinates that condition these corrections on each query. On balanced synthetic-speech PopQA, paired comparisons do not establish a clear advantage for AFFIRM over global SALSA steering or low-rank ReFT interventions under the same frozen single-state constraint. On recorded SLUE-SQA-5 questions with text passages, a full-scale PopQA-fitted AFFIRM operator transfers without refitting and improves ROUGE-L by 0.0941 over its paired No Repair baseline. Low-rank ReFT interventions also transfer positively, whereas the matched SALSA variant falls 0.0707 below its paired baseline. These results show that text acquisition and speech access can diverge after an update and should be verified and repaired separately.

Evaluating perceptual transparency of lossy music audio compression with Opus codec2610.20246v1其他
展开摘要

Although the open-source Opus audio codec is widely used for digital audio streaming, its perceptual transparency threshold for high-bitrate music compression remains insufficiently evaluated. To establish this threshold, this study evaluates Opus across a two-phase ITU-R BS.1116-3 double-blind framework, analyzing Difference Grades using a Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM) and Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for statistical equivalence. In a first phase with 21 qualified listeners and 23 music excerpts, 128 kbps achieves statistical in-distinguishability ($p = 0.456$) but narrowly fails strict equivalence to the uncompressed reference ($p = 0.064$). In Phase 2, Best Linear Unbiased Predictors (BLUPs) isolate the 10 most critical listeners and 8 most challenging samples for a stress-test. Under these worst-case conditions, 128 kbps is conclusively detected as degraded ($p = 0.030$), whereas 160 kbps successfully reaches perceptual transparency ($p = 0.747$) and strict statistical equivalence ($p = 0.024$). The study concludes that 160 kbps demonstrates perceptual transparency to uncompressed CD quality in critical listening environments. For reproducibility, we publicly release results for both phases, anonymized subject background info and sample characteristics.

Noise-Aware Lombard Speech Synthesis via Flow-GRPO for Intelligibility Optimization2610.20181v1语音合成
展开摘要

Voice-based conversational agents are increasingly deployed in real-world settings, where environmental noise often degrades the interactive experience. Therefore, robust acoustic adaptability is essential to maintain natural and intelligible human-computer communication. However, most existing systems remain acoustically insensitive and fail to emulate the human Lombard effect, in which speakers involuntarily adjust their vocal production to preserve intelligibility in the presence of noise. As a result, their communicative reliability degrades under adverse acoustic conditions. To remain intelligible in noisy environments, conversational agents require a mechanism to emulate noise-induced adaptive behavior during synthesis. We propose NIL-TTS, a noise-induced Lombard TTS framework. We integrate a feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) mechanism into a flow-matching backbone for dynamic vocal adaptation. A two-stage training paradigm is utilized: initial supervised fine-tuning on paired Lombard corpora, followed by Flow-GRPO post-training to optimize for intelligibility. Evaluations confirm that NIL-TTS autonomously adjusts its output in response to varying noise conditions, exhibiting key correlates of the human Lombard effect. Driven by these adaptive modulations, the synthesized speech achieves significant intelligibility improvements that scale proportionally with noise intensity. Ultimately, this work presents a flow-matching and reinforcement learning-based advancement within the adaptive Lombard speech synthesis paradigm for challenging real-world scenarios.

Anuvani-Diarization: Zero Shot Speaker Diarization and Annotation Conventions Across 22 Languages2610.20327v1说话人
展开摘要

India's 22 scheduled languages are spoken by over a billion people in conversations that mix languages, often within one turn, overlap freely, and take place over telephones, in meetings, and in media. Knowing who spoke when, the task of speaker diarization, is essential to speech applications for such conversation: voice agents conversing with several people at once, speaker attributed transcription of meetings and calls, contact center analytics, and media monitoring. We present a systematic study of speaker diarization across all 22 languages on IndicDiarBench and introduce Anuvani-Diarization, which adapts a self-supervised end-to-end segmentation model to Indian conversation with uncertainty aware silver supervision. Evaluated zero shot alongside nine recent diarization systems, it reaches a duration weighted diarization error rate (DER) of 16.78% on all 1,164 recordings, the lowest of all systems we ran and second only to the best published result (16.0%), and leads the three strongest rivals in 14 of 22 languages. It matches its parent on six international benchmarks. We also show that the benchmark's utterance level annotation convention is a hidden variable worth 1.9 to 4.1 DER points, depending on the system, and that a convention learned from other Indian transcriptions does not transfer.

MUSEBENCH: A Benchmark for AI-Generated Music Detection and Artifact Analysis2610.20310v1其他
展开摘要

Advances in generative AI have made it possible to produce music that is nearly indistinguishable from human compositions, raising the question of whether such works can be reliably distinguished from human compositions. We present MUSEBENCH, a large-scale benchmark for AI-generated music detection and artifact analysis, comprising over 361K tracks. The dataset includes human compositions, stylistically matched music generated by ten state-of-the-art open-source and commercial models, such as Suno and Udio, as well as human-AI co-creations and variants processed with adversarial perturbations, compression, and acoustic post-processing. Using MUSEBENCH, we benchmark 11 automated detectors and evaluate their accuracy, generalization, and robustness, under default, out-of-domain, and transformed-variant experiment settings. We further conduct a comprehensive artifact analysis through acoustic feature inspection, identifying recurring signatures such as non-idiomatic harmonic progressions, upsampling induced spectral periodicities, and violations of physical performance constraints. Our findings reveal key strengths and vulnerabilities of existing detectors and provide practical insights for building more reliable AI music detection systems.

Anuvani-LID: Robust and Calibrated Spoken Language Identification for Indian Languages2610.20263v1其他
展开摘要

Every multilingual speech pipeline begins with a language decision, an error there propagates silently through recognition, translation and routing. In India it spans 22 scheduled languages across four language families, heard over short, noisy, narrowband and code-mixed audio. Foundational speech models increasingly present language identification (LID) as an inherent latent, generalized capability. Autoregressive decoders in speech models emit a special language ID token, and modern LLMs with audio capabilities can name the language when prompted. We test this assumption on identical clips and find that whisper and similar speech models has tokens for only 15 of the 23 languages, on one second inputs Gemini 3.5 Flash reaches 0.52 macro-F1 and GPT-audio 0.14. We present Anuvani LID, a dedicated 115M parameter LID conformer model trained on 54,643 hours of Indian speech spanning 22 Indian languages and Indian accented English. Along with designed and controlled studies on 102 condition benchmark with speaker and corpus held out tests. Across 37 conditions, it reaches 0.914 mean macro-F1, 5.2 points above Gemini 3.5 Flash and ahead of 14 other systems. It is 24 points ahead of Gemini at one second, scores 0.897 on all 23 languages where no other system exceeds 0.713, and is calibrated to an ECE of 0.008. Reliable Indian LID still requires a robust, dedicated and calibrated model.

Language captures only a subset of the perceptions, intuitions and intents that form meaning between speakers. What, then, are the limits of language? We take steps towards measuring this in LLMs as follows: for a set of concepts, we measure how well each concept can be transmitted between two LLMs as their capabilities scale. This requires novel measurement tools: (1) optimal messages: we introduce a prompt optimization algorithm that beats existing algorithms by up to 14% on benchmarks and accurately estimates prompt upper bounds. (2) A transmission measure: the sender is asked to label text; the receiver then tries to recover the sender's prompt, and recovery is measured; we find this correlates strongly with human label error. With these measures, we use observational scaling laws to study how transmissibility of a concept, conveyed with optimal messages, increases as transmitter and receiver capacity grows: does it asymptote below or above a threshold? Measuring 1.2k concepts in eight domains, we find concepts that (a) can be fully articulated; these concepts usually depend on local patterns, e.g. "joke ends with surprise"; (b) might be articulated by future, stronger models; these rely on whole-text structural patterns, e.g. "story's worldbuilding coherence"; (c) concepts that face articulability limits; these concepts' meaning, we find, is tied to identity and social-group awareness, e.g. "comic voice". Interesting, these same concepts are not all challenging for humans: humans have moderate-to-high levels of agreement in 76% of concepts that LLMs struggle with; articulation limits, we find, are executor relative. Finally, we characterize the nature of improvement in each case: while recoverable concepts can be articulated through definitions and explanations, irrecoverable concepts cannot be transmitted, even with examples, and might require novel learning mechanisms.
