VocalGrad: Evaluating Acoustic Perception in Audio Language Models

作者：Koki Ryu, Hitomi Yanaka

其他评分 14speechaudioacoustic查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

论文提出VocalGrad基准，通过二分类时间变化方向来评估音频语言模型的声学感知能力，发现LALMs表现接近随机并揭示两种失败模式。

解决了什么问题

大型音频语言模型在可靠利用声学信息方面存在困难，特别是当声学信息与语言内容同时出现时；现有基准未能区分声学感知与高级推理，难以定位失败原因。

核心方法

构建VocalGrad基准，将声学感知评估形式化为时间变化方向的二分类任务，并结合线性探测分析模型内部表示。

主要创新点

首次明确区分声学感知与高级推理，设计低层声学变化分类任务；通过线性探测揭示模型内部两种失败模式。

实验结论

人类标注者在该基准上准确率高，而最先进的LALMs表现接近随机水平；线性探测显示部分类别信息在内部表示中保留但未被语言模型头利用，另一些类别信息随层数增加逐渐不可线性解码。

可能局限

摘要中未明确说明

适合谁读

研究音频语言模型、语音处理、模型评估与可解释性的学者和工程师。

和已有工作的关系

摘要中提到现有基准未区分声学感知与高级推理，但未具体列举；相关工作涉及大型音频语言模型的能力评估。

对研究的启发

该基准设计提示可针对低层感知能力单独评估，避免高级推理干扰；失败模式分析可为改进音频语言模型内部信息利用提供线索。

是否值得精读

是，因为其揭示了当前音频语言模型的关键短板，且方法简单有效。

Jarvis: Coordination Deserves a Brain of Its Own in Agentic Interactive Models

作者：Yuxiang Wang, Shengbo Cai, Zhixian Zhao, Yingda Shen, Yuancheng Wang, Xueyao Zhang, Liqiang Zhang, Teddy Sun, Steve Yves, Zhizheng Wu

其他评分 5speechaudiovoice查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

论文提出Jarvis协调框架，通过独立的决策模型和代码执行来协调前端对话模型与后端任务，显著提高语音代理任务成功率。

解决了什么问题

交互模型将复杂推理交给后台慢速大脑，前端模型只跟踪对话，导致用户修改请求时计划和结果容易过时，缺乏协调两者的大脑。

核心方法

引入Jarvis协调层，包含类型化决策模型JEV和代码执行检查，基于版本化任务记录选择操作，并在执行前和播放前重新检查。

主要创新点

提出独立协调“大脑”概念，让模型判断、代码执行保证一致性；设计版本化任务记录和交付确认机制；实例化Jarvis-Audio和Jarvis-Omni。

实验结论

在固定前后端模型条件下，用Jarvis替换Qwen Audio Agent使tau-Voice任务成功率从29/278提升至192/278，提高超过六倍；Jarvis-Audio在Full-Duplex-Bench v3上达到69% Pass1，优于GPT-Realtime的60%。

作者：Séverin Baroudi, Yanis Labrak, Petr Motlicek, Hervé Bredin, Ricard Marxer

语音合成评分 4speechaudiovoice查看 arXiv ↗

论文系统评估Qwen3-TTS的两种克隆接口在不同领域语音上的表现，发现接口无绝对优势，领域差异是关键，并揭示其在韵律和ATC通信上的失败。

解决了什么问题

Qwen3-TTS技术报告未明确如何条件化模型、在朗读语音之外的表现和失败模式，缺乏系统性评估。

核心方法

对比Qwen3-TTS的ICL和X-Vector接口与Chatterbox、XTTS-v2、F5-TTS，在朗读、会议、情感和ATC语音上使用十八种客观指标、LLM裁判、欺骗对策和听感测试。

主要创新点

首次对Qwen3-TTS进行跨领域多系统基准测试，结合客观、主观和安全指标，公开代码和音频。

没有一个接口全面占优：ICL在正确转录参考下克隆音色更接近，X-Vector克隆时间更好，ICL在短参考转录错误时急剧下降；领域因素解释方差是系统选择的四倍；Qwen3-TTS在可懂度和音高上领先但韵律落后；客观指标与听者排名不一致；欺骗对策未将其克隆标记为高于真实录音；所有系统在ATC语音上均失败。

解决了什么问题

VocalGrad: Evaluating Acoustic Perception in Audio Language Models2610.23745v1其他
展开摘要

Recent large audio language models (LALMs) have extended their capabilities beyond lexical speech understanding to tasks requiring paralinguistic and non-linguistic audio understanding, such as emotion recognition. However, these models still struggle to reliably utilize acoustic information, particularly when it co-occurs with spoken linguistic content. Existing benchmarks do not clearly distinguish acoustic perception from higher-level reasoning, making it difficult to identify the source of these failures. To address this issue, we introduce VocalGrad, a benchmark designed to evaluate LALMs' ability to perceive low-level acoustic variations. VocalGrad formulates this problem as a simple binary classification of temporal change direction, enabling evaluation largely independent of higher-level reasoning or semantic understanding. Our benchmark results reveal a substantial gap in acoustic perception between human annotators and state-of-the-art LALMs: while human annotators achieve high accuracy, LALMs perform at near-chance levels. We further conduct linear probing to identify structural bottlenecks in these models. Our analysis reveals two distinct failure modes: for some categories, task-relevant information is preserved in internal representations but not utilized by the language model head; for others, the information becomes progressively less linearly decodable across model layers. Overall, our results reveal substantial limitations of LALMs in modeling acoustic variation under controlled settings and expose representation differences not observable from text outputs.

Jarvis: Coordination Deserves a Brain of Its Own in Agentic Interactive Models2610.23804v1其他
展开摘要

Interactive models increasingly hand complex reasoning and tool use to a slow brain in the background, leaving a realtime front model free to sustain full-duplex dialogue. Yet the user may refine, cancel or replace a request while its task runs, so a plan or result that made sense a moment ago goes stale. Each new utterance must therefore be read against the work in flight, but the front model follows only the conversation and the back end sees only its own task. Coordination deserves a brain of its own, one that sees both. We introduce Jarvis, a coordination harness where models judge and code enforces. Its typed decision model, JEV, reads each turn against the live tasks and picks among operations built from a versioned task record. Code checks every choice against the current state before applying it, so no decision can act on a task version the user has replaced. Jarvis also rechecks plans before they execute and results before they are spoken, and counts an answer as delivered only once the user has heard it in full. We build two instances of this design. Jarvis-Audio runs unchanged on three full-duplex speech models. Jarvis-Omni adds vision and rechecks the evidence behind an answer before and during playback, since a scene can change while nobody speaks. With the front model and back end held fixed, replacing Qwen Audio Agent with Jarvis raises tau-Voice task success more than sixfold, from 29 to 192 of 278 tasks, and the gains hold on every benchmark and front model. Against published systems, Jarvis-Audio reaches 69% Pass1 on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 with an open 9B front model, versus 60% for GPT-Realtime. These results suggest that how the two brains are coordinated matters as much as how strong they are.

Beyond the Benchmark: Conditioning, Domains and Failure Modes of Qwen3-TTS2610.23678v1语音合成
展开摘要

Qwen3-TTS reports state-of-the-art zero-shot voice cloning, but its technical report leaves open how the model should be conditioned, how it behaves outside read speech, and how it fails. We characterise its two cloning interfaces, in-context learning (ICL) and X-Vector, against Chatterbox, XTTS-v2 and F5-TTS on read, meeting, emotional and air-traffic-control (ATC) speech, using eighteen objective measures, an LLM judge, a spoofing countermeasure and a listening test. Neither interface dominates: ICL clones timbre more closely from correctly transcribed references, X-Vector clones timing, and ICL degrades sharply when a short reference transcript is wrong. Domain explains four times more variance than system choice. Qwen3-TTS leads on intelligibility and pitch register but trails on prosody, and the objective measures rank systems differently from listeners. An off-the-shelf countermeasure flags its clones no more often than genuine recordings, and on ATC radio it fails for every system. Code and audio are released.

Bridging the Language Gap in Scholarly Data II: Enhancing Gender Inference for Chinese Names2610.23734v1其他
展开摘要

Perceived gender inference from names is important for inclusive scientometrics research, yet existing methods perform poorly on non-Western scholars. We address this gap for Chinese names, which appear in two forms, Hanzi and Pinyin, its Romanized form (e.g., Zhang Wei, Li Na). In international bibliographic records, names are often available only in Pinyin. However, a single pinyin name can correspond to multiple distinct hanzi characters with different gender associations, so gender inference from Pinyin alone is intrinsically ambiguous. To address this, we first develop two hanzi models, a regression model that produces a continuous gender score from 0 (male) to 1 (female), and a classification model that outputs one of three labels (\em{male}, \em{neutral}, \em{female}). We train them on a sample of hanzi names from Chinese-language physics publications, rated by 11 native Chinese speakers. On this sample, the models achieve an RMSE of $0.09$ and an R$^2$ of $0.85$ (regression), and macro-AUC of $0.97$ and a Cohen's $κ$ of $0.81$ (classification), demonstrating high performance that is robust to class imbalance. For pinyin names, we propose a pipeline that aggregates hanzi predictions via transliteration and frequency weighting, together with an ambiguity score that flags names whose gender cannot be resolved from Pinyin alone. Benchmarked against three off-the-shelf tools (Namsor, GPT, and Claude), our models achieve the highest agreement with human annotations for both gender scores and gender labels, in both scripts. Applied to an independent sample of Chinese-named authors in APS journals, our models assign a gender to every name, whereas existing Pinyin-based methods leave between 20\% and 100\% of names unclassified. These findings demonstrate the importance of hanzi-level information for reliable gender inference of Chinese names. Code and data are publicly available.
