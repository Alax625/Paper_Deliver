The SLT 2026 SmartGlasses Challenge: Benchmarking Egocentric Multi-Talker Speech Recognition and Understanding with Audio-Language Models

作者：Dehui Gao, Zhixian Zhao, Zhennan Lin, Yujie Liao, Yuhang Dai, Yike Zhu, Longshuai Xiao, Hui Bu, Xin Xu, Xie Chen, Shuai Wang, Liumeng Xue, Zhonghua Fu, Jun Du, Eng-Siong Chng, Jun Zhou, Lei Xie

语音识别评分 24speechaudioacousticspeakerspeech recognition查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

引入IEEE SLT 2026智能眼镜挑战赛，基于106小时四通道第一视角语音数据集基准评测多说话人语音识别与理解。

解决了什么问题

智能眼镜场景下语音识别和理解面临动态声学条件、说话人重叠、佩戴者中心录音几何导致的空间模糊等挑战，缺乏系统评测基准。

核心方法

构建挑战赛基准，包含双人对话理解和多方会议理解两个赛道，联合评估时间戳说话人归属自动语音识别（TSA-ASR）和口语理解（SLU），使用106小时、714个真实世界会话的四通道第一视角语音数据集。

主要创新点

首次针对智能眼镜第一视角多说话人场景建立系统评测基准；联合评估TSA-ASR和SLU；提供真实世界多通道数据集。

实验结论

摘要指出，严重说话人重叠仍是影响TSA-ASR的主要因素；副语言声学理解对当前音频语言模型在复杂SLU设置中依然困难。

可能局限

摘要中未明确说明。

适合谁读

语音识别、可穿戴设备、多说话人语音处理、口语理解研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要提及大语言模型和MLLMs的进展为可穿戴语音接口创造机会，但未展开详细相关工作。

对研究的启发

针对说话人重叠的处理方法以及提升副语言声学理解能力是潜在研究方向；新基准可用于评估相关模型。

是否值得精读

提供了新的基准数据集和评测发现，对第一视角语音识别和SLU研究者有一定参考价值。

VoxAudio: Vocalized Audio Synthesis via Multi-Reward Autoregressive Flow Matching

作者：Wenxiang Guo, Changhao Pan, Ziyue Jiang, Fei Wu, Zhou Zhao

音频生成评分 20speechaudio查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

提出VoxAudio因果自回归流匹配模型，统一生成嵌入可懂语音的环境音景，并引入多奖励微调和带时间标注的数据集。

解决了什么问题

现有Text-to-Audio系统要么将引用语音降级为不可理解的 murmur，要么分离TTS后混合，丧失对语音发生时机和场景交互的控制。

核心方法

采用因果自回归流匹配模型；架构上使用分块因果分解、每块独立噪声水平，支持滑窗流式推理与KV缓存；通过随机块边界预训练支持任意块粒度；偏好优化使用多奖励Negative-aware FineTuning联合优化语义保真、语言准确、美学质量和时间grounding；构建VoxCorpus和VoxBench数据集。

主要创新点

支持任意时长和任意块粒度的流式推理；多奖励微调同时优化多个目标；构建包含逐字转录和时间区间的VoxCorpus及带区间标注的VoxBench。

实验结论

摘要在四个基准上的实验验证了VoxAudio的有效性和效率，未给出具体指标数值。

可能局限

摘要中未明确说明。

适合谁读

音频生成、语音合成、多模态内容生成、播客制作/视频配音相关研究人员。

和已有工作的关系

摘要指出已有T2A系统将引用语音降级为 murmur 或采用分离TTS后混合的方案，未列出具体工作。

对研究的启发

时间区间标注和多奖励微调可提升生成音频中语音与场景的同步性和可控性；流式分块架构为长音频生成提供思路。

是否值得精读

提出新的统一框架和数据集，在音频生成方向有一定创新，值得关注。

Drive-to-Music: Context-Aware Generative Audio for In-Vehicle Experiences

作者：Cosmin Dragoiu, Nooshin Nabizadeh

音乐生成评分 16audiomusicmusic generation查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

提出Drive-to-Music系统，利用行车记录仪影像和车辆遥测实时生成与驾驶情境一致的背景音乐。

解决了什么问题

车内音乐需要适应驾驶环境以增强体验，但缺乏将多模态驾驶信号实时映射到音乐生成的方法。

主要创新点

将视觉和运动输入实时转化为结构化音乐属性；支持平滑过渡；引入约束控制和安全检查确保部署稳健性。

实验结论

结果证明在汽车环境中实时上下文感知音乐生成的可行性（摘要原文），未提供具体指标。

摘要中未明确说明。

适合谁读

音乐生成、人机交互、车载系统、多模态生成研究者。

和已有工作的关系

对研究的启发

将多模态驾驶信号映射到高级音乐描述符、并加入约束与安全检查的思路，可为其他实时生成场景提供参考。

Rethinking Language Model-Based Generative Speech Enhancement in the Latent Space of a Neural Audio Codec

作者：Yihui Fu, Zhengyang Li, Tim Fingscheidt

语音增强评分 15speechaudiospeech enhancement查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

系统比较了六种基于语言模型的生成式语音增强范式，并提出辅助损失微调策略以提升性能。

解决了什么问题

语言模型语音增强范式多样但缺乏统一比较框架和公平评估；需要提升各范式的语音质量和可懂度指标。

核心方法

统一框架涵盖六种基于离散/连续神经音频编解码器潜在特征的生成式语音增强建模范式（离散/连续自回归、离散/连续非自回归、离散扩散、连续流匹配）；在URGENT 2025数据上统一评估；提出对重构语音施加辅助损失的微调策略。

主要创新点

首次在统一设置下公平比较六种LM生成语音增强范式；提出辅助损失微调策略；发现连续域范式普遍优于离散域，CNAR最佳。

实验结论

在URGENT 2025数据上，所有连续域范式优于离散域，最佳为CNAR；辅助损失微调一致改进DNSMOS、NISQA、PESQ、POLQA。

适合谁读

和已有工作的关系

摘要梳理了六种LM生成语音增强范式，未详细对比其他工作。

对研究的启发

连续潜在特征的生成式语音增强和辅助损失微调是值得探索的方向；统一评估框架有助于系统比较。

是否值得精读

涉及统一比较与改进策略，对从事生成式语音增强研究者有参考价值。

Evaluating Pre-trained Speech Encoders for Spontaneous Speech Detection and Out of Domain Synthetic Speech Generalisation in Indic Languages

作者：Varun Rai, Pavan Kumar J, Sujith Pulikodan, Nihar Desai

语音合成评分 12speech查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

在22种Indic语言上评估五种预训练语音编码器在自发性检测和合成语音泛化上的表现，并用嵌入几何分析解释行为。

解决了什么问题

自发与照稿、自然与合成语音检测在Indic语言上未充分评估；编码器行为及深度伪造泛化失败缺乏嵌入几何解释。

核心方法

评估五个冻结transformer编码器（AST、Vaani-FastConformer、Wav2vec2、Whisper、BEATs）在22种Indic语言上的表现；进行四个TTS模型的泛化实验；提出语言隔离探测和质心邻近分析。

主要创新点

扩展到Indic语言；引入语言隔离探测和质心邻近分析解释编码器行为和域外泛化；发现泛化由训练系统与未见TTS嵌入的接近度预测。

实验结论

探测发现编码器依赖的语言可分性与自发性检测之间存在权衡；质心分析显示域外泛化由训练系统与未见TTS嵌入的接近度预测，而非与自然语音的距离。

可能局限

摘要中未明确说明。

适合谁读

语音表征学习、伪造语音检测、低资源语言语音处理研究者。

和已有工作的关系

摘要指出先前transformer模型在区分类别上表现强但基于窄基准，未扩展至Indic语言及嵌入几何分析。

对研究的启发

嵌入几何分析可为深度伪造检测训练数据选择提供指导；语言可分性与任务性能的权衡值得进一步研究。

是否值得精读

对多语言语音检测和深度伪造泛化有分析价值，值得关注。

The SLT 2026 SmartGlasses Challenge: Benchmarking Egocentric Multi-Talker Speech Recognition and Understanding with Audio-Language Models2608.12034v1语音识别
展开摘要

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) have created new opportunities for wearable speech interfaces, with smart glasses providing an egocentric platform for continuous audio sensing and assistance. However, speech recognition and understanding in this setting remain challenging because of dynamic acoustic conditions, speaker overlap, and the spatial ambiguity introduced by wearer-centered recording geometry. To support systematic evaluation in this setting, we introduce the IEEE SLT 2026 SmartGlasses Challenge for egocentric multi-speaker speech processing. The challenge consists of two tracks, Dyadic Dialogue Understanding and Multi-party Meeting Understanding, and jointly evaluates Time-Stamped Speaker-Attributed Automatic Speech Recognition (TSA-ASR) and Spoken Language Understanding (SLU). It is built on a 106-hour four-channel egocentric speech dataset containing 714 sessions collected in real-world scenarios. This paper describes challenge tasks, dataset construction, submissions, and summarizes the main findings from the shared evaluation. The results show that heavy speaker overlap remains a major factor affecting TSA-ASR performance, while paralinguistic acoustic understanding continues to be difficult for current audio-language models in complex SLU settings. Further details can be found on the official challenge website.

VoxAudio: Vocalized Audio Synthesis via Multi-Reward Autoregressive Flow Matching2608.12951v1音频生成
展开摘要

Vocalized audio synthesis, the task of generating audio in which intelligible speech is embedded within an environmental soundscape, underpins applications such as podcast production and video dubbing. Existing Text-to-Audio (T2A) systems either reduce quoted speech to unintelligible vocal murmur or delegate it to a separate TTS model with post-hoc mixing, which forfeits control over when speech occurs and how it interacts with the scene. We present VoxAudio, a causal autoregressive flow matching model that addresses this problem from three complementary aspects. At the architecture level, chunk-wise causal factorization with independent per-chunk noise levels lets audio be emitted through sliding-window streaming inference with KV caching at variable target durations; to enable inference at arbitrary chunk granularities, we further pretrain the model with randomized chunk boundaries. At the preference level, multi-reward Negative-aware FineTuning (NFT) jointly optimizes semantic fidelity, linguistic accuracy, aesthetic quality, and temporal grounding At the data level, to supply the missing supervision for vocal content, we build VoxCorpus, a large-scale corpus whose captions quote the verbatim transcript of embedded speech with time intervals, and VoxBench, an interval-annotated benchmark with a temporal-grounding metric. Experiments on four benchmarks spanning general audio, speech, and unified vocalized audio validate the effectiveness and efficiency of VoxAudio. Our code and demos are available at https://voxaudio.github.io.

Drive-to-Music: Context-Aware Generative Audio for In-Vehicle Experiences2608.12615v1音乐生成
展开摘要

In-vehicle music can serve as an adaptive interface to enhance driver experience, attention, and well-being. We present Drive-to-Music, a context-aware system that generates music in real time from multimodal driving signals. Using dashcam imagery and vehicle telemetry, the system extracts scene semantics and driving context, maps them to high-level musical descriptors, and conditions generative audio models to produce contextually aligned soundtracks. The architecture combines perception and generative components to translate visual and kinematic inputs into structured musical attributes and synthesize audio with low latency. It supports smooth transitions as driving conditions evolve, and to ensure robustness and deployment readiness, we incorporate constraint-based controls and safety checks across the generation pipeline. Our results demonstrate the feasibility of real-time, context-aware music generation in automotive settings, providing a foundation for personalized and adaptive in-vehicle audio experiences.

Rethinking Language Model-Based Generative Speech Enhancement in the Latent Space of a Neural Audio Codec2608.12082v1语音增强
展开摘要

Language model (LM)-based speech enhancement (SE) has recently emerged rapidly using latent space features of neural audio codecs (NACs). In this paper, first, we present a unified framework covering six popular LM-based generative SE modeling paradigms based on discrete/continuous latent NAC features: discrete or continuous autoregressive (D/CAR) SE, discrete or continuous non-autoregressive (D/CNAR) SE, discrete diffusion (DDiff) SE, and continuous flow matching (CFM) SE. Second, we are the first to compare their performance in a unified experimental setup and synopsis with diverse intrusive and non-intrusive metrics, enabling a fair and comprehensive evaluation. Third, we propose a fine-tuning strategy with auxiliary losses on reconstructed speech to improve both intrusive and non-intrusive metrics. Trained and evaluated on URGENT 2025 Speech Enhancement Challenge data splits, all continuous-domain paradigms excel their discrete-domain counterparts. The overall best approach turns out to be CNAR. We further show that our proposed auxiliary loss fine-tuning strategy helps to improve DNSMOS, NISQA, PESQ, and POLQA consistently in all six paradigms.

Evaluating Pre-trained Speech Encoders for Spontaneous Speech Detection and Out of Domain Synthetic Speech Generalisation in Indic Languages2608.12536v1语音合成
展开摘要

Transformer-based models have shown strong accuracy in distinguishing spontaneous from scripted speech and natural from synthetic speech, but these results are established on a narrow set of well-resourced language benchmarks and have not been extended across Indic languages, nor has embedding geometry been used to explain encoder behaviour or deepfake generalisation failure. We address these gaps by evaluating five frozen transformer encoders, AST, Vaani-FastConformer, Wav2vec2, Whisper and BEATs, across 22 Indic languages, and by conducting a multi-system TTS generalisation experiment across four TTS models. Beyond accuracy, we present language isolation probing and centroid proximity analysis. Probing reveals an encoder-dependent trade-off between language-discriminability and spontaneity detection. Centroid analysis shows that out-of-domain generalisation is predicted by a training system's proximity to unseen TTS embeddings, not its distance from natural speech, a finding with direct implications for training data selection in real-world deepfake detectors.

CASA: Content-Acoustic Speaking Assessment with Speech Encoder and Large Language Model2608.13101v1其他
展开摘要

Research on automatic speaking assessment (ASA) has increasingly adopted multimodal speech large language models to assess learners' speaking performance. However, existing studies provide limited analysis of how acoustic and content information contribute to predictions and how stable the resulting performance is. We propose CASA, a simpler architecture combining Whisper-medium and Qwen3.5-2B that achieves state-of-the-art performance while providing a more interpretable separation between speech delivery and content. On the Speak & Improve Corpus 2025, CASA achieves a root mean square error (RMSE) of 0.358, improving on the previous best RMSE while using approximately half the estimated inference parameters. The general-purpose architecture is designed for adaptation to other ASA corpora without structural changes and relies on three handcrafted fluency features. Through ablations and repeated runs, we analyze the individual and complementary contributions of acoustic and content information, examine performance variability, and demonstrate the potential of large language model reasoning for training-free content validation.

Speculative decoding speeds up generation by letting a cheap draft propose several tokens that a target model checks in one pass. In the single-model form, the draft is a lightweight module attached to the target rather than a separate model. Applying this design to Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) introduces an extra problem. The draft can read the whole audio at every step, yet its proposals get worse as it runs on its own. Access is not localization. The accepted text keeps the transcript position explicit, but the draft must also track the changing audio position. In the primary matched comparison, per-step audio access changes the first proposal modestly but roughly doubles later-proposal acceptance. Fixed-width windows show that the audio position explains part of this gap. A correctly placed window recovers continuation, while an equally narrow window at the wrong position reduces it. Late-draft median error reaches 21 frames in the hardest reported condition, while target attention during verification stays within a 2-frame median. We test two ways to reduce this drift. The first reads the audio position from verification attention and uses it to guide the next draft round. It saves time only when the extra accepted tokens offset the readout cost. The second is AnchorDraft, which teaches the draft to track the audio position during training without changing the inference graph. The trained draft improves end-to-end speed at both tested target scales. These results show that ASR self-speculation depends on token prediction, audio-position tracking, and draft cost.
