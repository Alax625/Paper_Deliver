Personalized Causal Low-Bitrate Coded Speech Restoration with Speaker Embeddings

作者：Eray Özgünay, Christian Rollwage, Kishan Gupta, Andreas Brendel, Xavier Domont, Mattes Ohlenbusch, Jan Rennies, Simon Doclo

说话人评分 20speechacousticspeaker查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

提出两种基于说话人嵌入的个性化编码语音恢复方法，其中嵌入条件化在ViSQOL和WB-PESQ上一致优于非个性化基线，而聚类选择方法表现不稳定。

解决了什么问题

低码率语音编解码器（如AMR-NB）限制语音带宽并降低听感质量，传统编码语音恢复通常不针对不同说话人进行适配。

核心方法

以因果低复杂度通用带宽扩展生成对抗网络（UBGAN）为基础，提出两种个性化方法：单一CSR模型条件化于说话人嵌入；基于声学相似说话人聚类的多模型选择。推理时额外计算成本很小。

主要创新点

系统研究了低码率编码语音恢复中的个性化；利用说话人嵌入实现轻量级条件化；聚类方法提供另一种个性化思路。

实验结论

在4.75 kbps AMR-NB编码语音上，使用不相交的已见和未见说话人测试集，聚类模型选择未一致优于非个性化UBGAN基线，而嵌入条件化显著提高已见和未见说话人的ViSQOL和WB-PESQ分数。

可能局限

摘要中未明确说明

适合谁读

语音编码、语音恢复、说话人自适应、语音增强领域的研究人员。

和已有工作的关系

摘要提到低码率编解码器仍广泛用于电话通信，编码语音恢复旨在恢复带宽并提高质量，但通常缺乏说话人自适应。

对研究的启发

说话人嵌入条件化在低复杂度模型上即可有效提升个性化恢复效果，且计算开销小；聚类方法未必带来稳定增益，提示需要更精细的说话人建模或聚类策略。

是否值得精读

是，提供了编码语音恢复个性化的系统比较，嵌入条件化方法简单有效，对实际电话语音场景有参考价值。

Back to Mel: Learning to Bridge Fidelity and Perception in Generative Speech Enhancement

作者：Andong Li, Kun Zhou, Xiaodong Li, Chengshi Zheng

语音增强评分 17speechaudioacousticspeech enhancement查看 arXiv ↗

一句话总结

提出基于流匹配的BAME框架，重新利用Mel谱结构，结合子带感知Transformer、相位感知神经声码器和一步保真度-感知对齐策略，在多个语音增强基准上获得良好表现。

解决了什么问题

现有生成式语音增强方法难以在声学保真度、感知质量和处理效率之间取得平衡。

核心方法

以Mel谱作为结构化声学表示，设计子带感知Transformer（SaT）进行高效生成建模；提出相位感知神经声码器，融入退化相位先验和质量引导的双相位策略；提出一步保真度-感知对齐策略微调流模型。

主要创新点

重新审视Mel谱并设计高效的SaT，仅用2.9%参数超越331M DiT；相位感知声码器显式利用相位信息；一步对齐策略轻量有效。

实验结论

在DNS-Challenge、EARS、GSR和URGENT基准上，客观和主观结果均显示在多种侵入式和非侵入式评估指标上有良好性能（具体数值未给出）。

对研究的启发

实验结论

网络收敛到六个稳定质量维度：节奏、旋律、音频、作曲、结构和歌词，指向统一的用户研究方法论。

提出两阶段生成框架，结合CoT增强的SFT和带多维约束感知奖励的GRPO，在说话人数未知场景下显著降低联合多说话人ASR和说话人分离的错误率。

解决了什么问题

说话人数未知且输出结构严格时，联合多说话人ASR与说话人分离很困难；仅SFT对齐的SpeechLLM在密集重叠下易产生突发幻觉和格式错误。

Personalized Causal Low-Bitrate Coded Speech Restoration with Speaker Embeddings2611.22620v1说话人
展开摘要

Legacy low-bitrate speech codecs such as AMR-NB remain widely used in telephony, where they restrict speech bandwidth and degrade listening quality. Coded speech restoration (CSR) aims to restore the speech bandwidth while improving the overall quality of coded speech, but typically without adapting to different speakers. In this paper, we systematically investigate personalization for restoration of speech coded at low bitrates, using a causal, low-complexity Universal Bandwidth Extension Generative Adversarial Network (UBGAN) model as the basis. We propose two personalization methods based on speaker embeddings, which incur little additional computational cost at inference: (i) conditioning a single CSR model on speaker embeddings, and (ii) cluster-based model selection, where multiple personalized models are trained on clusters of acoustically similar speakers. Experiments are performed on AMR-NB coded speech at 4.75 kbps. Experimental results with disjoint seen- and unseen-speaker test sets show that cluster-based model selection does not consistently outperform the non-personalized UBGAN baseline, whereas embedding conditioning significantly improves ViSQOL and WB-PESQ scores for both seen and unseen speakers.

Back to Mel: Learning to Bridge Fidelity and Perception in Generative Speech Enhancement2611.22628v1语音增强
展开摘要

Recent generative techniques have facilitated the rapid proliferation of speech enhancement approaches built upon learnable compact latent representations and large generative backbones. Nevertheless, it remains inherently challenging to balance between acoustic fidelity, perceptual quality, and processing efficiency. In this paper, we revisit the Mel spectrum as a structured acoustic representation and propose BAME, a flow-matching-based framework that exploits the complementary priors for generative speech enhancement task. First, leveraging the explicit hierarchical frequency distribution of Mel spectrum, we elaborately devise a subband-aware Transformer (SaT) for efficient generative modeling. Using only 2.9% of the parameters, the proposed SaT achieves superior enhancement performance compared with a 331 M DiT backbone. Second, motivated by range-null decomposition theory of Mel filtering in the magnitude-spectral domain, we introduce a phase-aware neural vocoder that incorporates degraded-phase prior, together with a quality-guided dual-phase strategy to adaptively mitigate the effects of unreliable phase information. Third, we devise a simple yet effective one-step fidelity-perception alignment strategy that fine-tunes the flow model with complementary constraints from fidelity and perception domains. Extensive experiments are conducted on DNS-Challenge, EARS, GSR, and URGENT benchmarks, and both objective and subjective results demonstrate promising performance on various intrusive and non-intrusive evaluation metrics. These results further advocate the potential of exploiting structured acoustic representations for reliable and efficient generative speech enhancement. Audio samples and codes are available at https://github.com/AndongLi-speech/BAME.

Evaluating the Evaluation of Music Generation Models with Generative Psychometrics2611.21809v1音乐生成
展开摘要

Subjective user studies are one of the major methods for evaluating music generation models. However, subjective evaluation protocols vary widely and are rarely specified in enough detail to be reproduced, hindering comparison across studies. In this paper, we map subjective evaluation methods in ISMIR music generation papers from 2020-2025 and apply generative psychometrics to assess the stability of their items. We extract 284 items from the papers' methodological descriptions, using an LLM and manual validation to standardize them. We represent these items as contextual embeddings and apply Unique Variable Analysis to remove redundant, locally dependent items and Bootstrap Exploratory Graph Analysis to estimate their latent dimensions. Results show the network converges onto a taxonomy of six stable quality dimensions: Rhythm, Melody, Audio, Composition, Structure, and Lyrics, pointing to a unified methodology for conducting user studies to evaluate music generation models.

Beyond Mimicry: Constrained Exploration with GRPO for Joint Multi-Talker ASR and Diarization under Unknown Speaker Counts2611.22627v1语音识别
展开摘要

Joint multi-talker ASR with speaker attribution and timestamps is particularly challenging when the speaker count is unknown and the output must follow a strict structure. We observe that Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-only alignment of SpeechLLMs is brittle under dense overlaps, often leading to burst hallucinations and malformed speaker/timestamp tags. We propose a unified two-stage generative framework: (i) CoT-augmented SFT that infers the global speaker count before transcription, and (ii) GRPO-based constrained exploration guided by a Multi-dimensional Constraint-Aware Reward (MCAR) that directly optimizes permutation-invariant accuracy while enforcing counting, temporal, and structural constraints. On Libri3Mix and a Dynamic-Mix (2+3) set (speaker count unknown at test time), our model achieves 14.52%/9.24% cpWER and 1.95%/1.12% WDER, respectively, with GRPO yielding a 35%/54% relative cpWER reduction over SFT+CoT.

Beyond Co-Generation: Rethinking Jointness in Joint Audio-Video Generation2611.22857v1音频生成
展开摘要

Our aim is to advance the understanding of genuine audio-visual coordination in joint text-to-audio-video (T2AV) generation, with particular emphasis on semantic consistency. Evaluating audio-visual consistency in T2AV generation is nontrivial, as detailed text prompts obscure whether such consistency stems from genuine cross-modal coordination or merely from independent text following. To isolate the former, we introduce intrinsic jointness: the ability of audio and video to produce the same realization for details left underspecified by the prompt. Using semantically underspecified prompts (e.g., "musical instrument playing" rather than "electric guitar playing"), we investigate four recent models and reveal their limited intrinsic jointness. To address this limitation, we propose JointREPA, a simple representation alignment-based post-training method to facilitate cross-modal coordination. Since existing benchmarks use semantically detailed prompts, we introduce UNSAIDBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate intrinsic jointness under semantic underspecification. Experiments across two distinct models and three benchmarks, including UNSAIDBench, demonstrate that JointREPA consistently improves semantic consistency.

High-Fidelity Binarized Neural Vocoder2611.22593v1语音合成
展开摘要

Neural vocoders have achieved remarkable progress in high-fidelity waveform generation in recent years, but most existing methods still rely on full-precision operators, leading to non-negligible storage and computational costs during inference. Binary neural networks (BNNs) provide an alternative direction by quantizing weights and activations to 1 bit. However, directly applying binarization to neural vocoders is rather challenging as it inherently incurs severe information loss. To this end, we propose BiVoC, a BNN-oriented time-frequency neural vocoder for high-fidelity waveform generation. Building on our previous full-precision RNDVoC framework, BiVoC adapts the inverse-problem formulation, range-null decomposition, and fineto-coarse non-uniform subband modeling to the 1-bit setting. Specifically, we develop a simple binarized convolutional unit to provide stable time-frequency feature modeling under binary representations. Furthermore, we analyze the quality-complexity sensitivity of different modules and propose a mixed-precision design to retain quality-sensitive components in full precision while binarizing computation-dominant parts. Extensive experiments are conducted on LibriTTS and multiple out-of-distribution benchmarks. Both objective and subjective results demonstrate that BiVoC substantially outperforms BNN variants obtained by directly binarizing existing vocoders, while achieving competitive performance against several mainstream full-precision vocoder baselines under much lower theoretical computational cost. These results demonstrate the promising potential of BNNs in the audio generation field. Codes and pre-trained weights are available at https://github.com/Andong-Li-speech/BiVoC.

Nunchi: Ears Help Eyes in Multi-Party Conversations2611.23115v1其他
展开摘要

Identifying whom a person looks at is central to understanding multi-party conversations, yet gaze geometry alone becomes ambiguous as more people appear. Conversations offer cues that geometry lacks, such as who is speaking and what is said, but no gaze dataset provides them for many multi-party scenes. We introduce SORBET, a benchmark of 3,340 samples from 1,578 clips with synchronized speech, transcripts, and attended person labels, covering more multi-party scenes than existing gaze datasets. Prior gaze models struggle to use these cues, which come in different forms, must be linked to the right person, and can conflict with gaze geometry. We propose Nunchi, a framework that constructs a text prompt from the transcript and the outputs of gaze and speaker models, links every cue to the candidates with shared labels, and lets a vision-language model (VLM) weigh gaze geometry against conversational cues to identify the attended person. Nunchi outperforms the strongest existing method by 4.8 percentage points. Omni Models process audio, vision, and text within a unified language backbone, yet often produce different answers for semantically equivalent inputs across modalities. Existing representation-level analyses do not distinguish between information that predicts whether disagreement will occur and the representation whose perturbation affects the model's answer. In this paper, we estimate a low-dimensional Answer Subspace from answer-position representations and use it to separate answer-related components from the remaining representation. Across three Omni Models and two multimodal reasoning tasks, information predictive of cross-modal inconsistency lies mainly outside the Answer Subspace, concentrating in a reproducible two-dimensional representation that is not explained by a fixed modality offset. Matched interventions reveal distinct behavioral roles: perturbing the Answer Subspace substantially changes the model's predictions, whereas perturbing the disagreement representation, like a random subspace of the same dimensionality, leaves them almost unchanged. Our key finding is that cross-modal disagreement can be induced by misaligning modality-specific answer representations: perturbing the Answer Subspace for only one modality induces disagreement, whereas applying the same perturbation to both largely preserves agreement. Thus, the representation that predicts disagreement is distinct from the answer-level representation through which disagreement can be induced, showing that linear decodability does not imply behavioral efficacy.
